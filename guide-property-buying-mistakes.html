Common Property-Buying Mistakes

Practical mistakes buyers should watch out for before committing their money.

Why These Mistakes Matter

Buying property can be an exciting experience, but it is also a major financial decision. Small oversights can create unnecessary problems later.

Understanding common mistakes can help you slow down, ask better questions and carry out the appropriate checks before making a commitment.

1. Rushing Because of Pressure

Some buyers make decisions simply because they are afraid that a property will disappear if they do not pay immediately.

While some properties may have genuine deadlines or limited availability, pressure should not prevent you from carrying out reasonable checks before paying.

2. Paying Before Verifying the Property

One of the most important mistakes to avoid is committing money before understanding the property's ownership, documentation, location and transaction terms.

Carry out appropriate verification before making a significant payment.

3. Relying Only on Verbal Promises

Important promises should not exist only in conversations.

Where an agreement, payment plan, specification, timeline or other important condition matters to the transaction, make sure it is properly documented.

4. Looking Only at the Advertised Price

The advertised purchase price may not always represent the complete cost of acquiring or maintaining a property.

Ask about documentation, development, legal, service, registration and other applicable charges before making your decision.

5. Ignoring the Location

A beautiful property may not be suitable for your purpose if the location does not meet your needs.

Consider accessibility, infrastructure, surrounding development, proximity to important places and the intended use of the property.

6. Skipping Property Inspection

Where an inspection is possible and appropriate, do not rely entirely on pictures or videos.

Seeing the property or development can help you understand its actual location, surroundings, access and current condition.

7. Choosing a Property Without Understanding the Documents

Buyers should understand what documents are available and what those documents represent in the particular transaction.

If you do not understand a document or the legal interest being transferred, consider seeking independent professional advice.

8. Not Reading the Agreement

Do not sign an agreement simply because someone tells you that it is standard.

Read the terms carefully. Pay attention to payment obligations, timelines, cancellation provisions, responsibilities and other important conditions.

9. Ignoring Payment Deadlines

If you are purchasing through an instalment plan, understand the payment schedule and keep track of your obligations.

Ask what happens if a payment is delayed and make sure you understand any consequences stated in the agreement.

10. Failing to Keep Payment Records

Keep receipts, transfer confirmations, agreements, correspondence and other important records relating to your transaction.

Organised records can make it easier to confirm what you have paid and what remains outstanding.

11. Making a Decision Based Only on Expected Appreciation

Property values can change, but future appreciation is not guaranteed.

Do not make a purchase based solely on promises about how much the property will supposedly be worth in the future.

12. Failing to Consider Your Actual Goal

A property should fit your purpose.

Consider whether you are buying to live in the property, rent it out, operate a business, build later or hold the property for another purpose.

Common-Mistake Checklist

  • Do not rush because of unnecessary pressure.
  • Verify the property before making significant payments.
  • Get important terms properly documented.
  • Ask for the complete cost, not just the advertised price.
  • Evaluate the location carefully.
  • Inspect the property where possible.
  • Understand the relevant documents.
  • Read the agreement before signing.
  • Understand payment deadlines and consequences.
  • Keep complete payment and transaction records.
  • Do not rely on guaranteed appreciation claims.
  • Make sure the property fits your purpose.

Final Thought

A careful buyer is not necessarily a slow buyer. A careful buyer is someone who asks questions, verifies important information and understands the transaction before committing money.

Take the time to understand what you are buying and seek appropriate professional advice when necessary.
