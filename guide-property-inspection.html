Preparing for a Property Inspection

What to look for when visiting a property, estate or development site.

Why Property Inspection Matters

Pictures, videos and descriptions can help you discover a property, but seeing the property or development yourself can give you a better understanding of what you are considering.

An inspection allows you to look at the location, access, surroundings, development and other practical details before making a decision.

1. Know What You Are Going to Inspect

Before the inspection, understand the exact property or unit you are visiting. Know the property name, location, property type, size and any key specifications that have been presented to you.

2. Confirm the Location

Pay attention to the actual location of the property and its surrounding environment.

Look at access roads, nearby developments, important landmarks and the general character of the area.

3. Check Accessibility

Consider how easy it is to reach the property.

Look at the condition of the access road, available routes and whether access is practical during different weather or traffic conditions.

4. Observe the Surroundings

Look beyond the property itself. Observe neighbouring properties, drainage, electricity infrastructure, roads, commercial activity and other developments around the area.

These factors can affect how convenient the property may be for its intended use.

5. Inspect the Property or Development

If a building already exists, inspect its general condition, layout, finishes, access points and visible facilities.

If the property is part of a developing estate, ask about the current development stage and the infrastructure already provided.

6. Ask About Infrastructure

Ask what infrastructure is currently available and what infrastructure is planned.

Depending on the development, this may include roads, drainage, electricity, water, security and other estate facilities.

7. Ask Questions During the Inspection

Do not simply walk around and leave. Use the opportunity to ask questions about anything you do not understand.

Ask about development plans, payment terms, documentation, timelines, service charges and other matters relevant to your decision.

8. Take Notes

It can be difficult to remember everything you see and discuss during an inspection, especially when visiting several properties.

Take notes about important observations, questions and answers so you can compare your options afterwards.

9. Take Photos or Videos Where Permitted

Where photography or recording is permitted, taking a few pictures or short videos can help you remember the property and discuss it with family members or advisers later.

10. Do Not Feel Pressured to Decide Immediately

An inspection is an opportunity to gather information. You do not have to make a rushed decision simply because you have visited the property.

Take time to review what you have seen, compare the information and ask any remaining questions before committing your money.

Property Inspection Checklist

  • Confirm the exact property and location.
  • Check access roads and routes.
  • Observe the surrounding environment.
  • Inspect the building or development where applicable.
  • Ask about available infrastructure.
  • Ask about planned development.
  • Clarify payment and additional costs.
  • Ask about documentation and verification.
  • Take notes of important observations.
  • Review everything before making a decision.

Final Thought

A property inspection is more than simply looking at a building or piece of land. It is an opportunity to gather practical information and determine whether the property matches your expectations and objectives.

Ask questions, observe carefully and take your time before making a major financial commitment.
