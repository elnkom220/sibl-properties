Understanding Property Documents in Nigeria

A simple guide to understanding some of the documents buyers may encounter.

Why Property Documents Matter

Property documents provide important information about a property, its history, ownership or interests in the property, and transactions relating to it. Before buying, it is important to understand what documents are being presented and what they actually establish.

1. Certificate of Occupancy (C of O)

A Certificate of Occupancy is a document associated with a right of occupancy over land. It is commonly discussed in Nigerian property transactions, but buyers should not assume that every property must have a C of O in the same form or that seeing a document alone proves that a transaction is safe.

2. Right of Occupancy (R of O)

A Right of Occupancy is another form of interest in land recognised under Nigeria's land administration framework. The terminology and documentation encountered can depend on the circumstances and the relevant authority.

3. Deed of Assignment

A Deed of Assignment is commonly used to document the transfer or assignment of an interest in property from one party to another. It is important that the parties, property and transaction are properly identified and that the document is reviewed and processed as required.

4. Survey Plan

A survey plan provides information about the location, boundaries and dimensions of a parcel of land. It can be an important document when establishing exactly which piece of land a transaction relates to.

5. Approved Building Documents

For developments involving buildings, buyers may also encounter building plans, planning approvals or other development-related documents. These should be considered separately from documents relating to the underlying land title.

6. Receipts and Transaction Records

Receipts, payment schedules, agreements and other transaction records can help establish what has been paid, what was agreed and the parties involved. Keep copies of important records throughout the transaction.

Documents Should Be Verified

A document should not be judged by its name or appearance alone. Property verification can involve checking records, ownership, boundaries, encumbrances, planning matters and other issues relevant to the particular transaction.

For a significant transaction, consider having the relevant documents reviewed by a qualified property lawyer and, where appropriate, a surveyor or other professional.

Questions to Ask

  • Who holds the interest in the property?
  • What document establishes that interest?
  • Does the document relate to the exact property being offered?
  • Can the relevant information be independently verified?
  • Are there restrictions, disputes or other interests affecting the property?
  • Are there additional approvals or documents required for the transaction?

Final Thought

Understanding property documents is an important part of making an informed property decision. Do not rely solely on a document shown by a seller or agent. Ask questions, verify relevant information and obtain professional advice where necessary.

Important: This guide is for general educational purposes only. Property documentation, verification procedures and land administration can vary depending on the location and circumstances. It does not replace professional legal, surveying or other specialised advice.
