Understanding Location and Property Value

Why location matters when evaluating a property and its long-term potential.

Why Location Matters

Location is one of the factors buyers consider when evaluating property. Two properties with similar physical characteristics can have very different values because of where they are located.

However, location should not be considered in isolation. The surrounding environment, accessibility, infrastructure, demand, development and the property's actual characteristics also matter.

1. Accessibility

Consider how easily people can reach the property.

Access roads, transportation routes, traffic patterns and proximity to major roads can influence how convenient a property is for residents, businesses or visitors.

2. Proximity to Important Places

Consider the property's distance from places that matter to its intended users.

Depending on your purpose, these may include schools, hospitals, workplaces, shopping areas, transportation hubs, recreational facilities and other important destinations.

3. Infrastructure

Infrastructure can have a significant effect on the usefulness and attractiveness of an area.

Consider roads, drainage, electricity, water supply, telecommunications and other infrastructure that is available or planned.

4. Development Around the Area

Look at what is happening around the property.

New residential developments, commercial activity, public infrastructure and other forms of development can change the character and usefulness of an area over time.

5. Demand

Consider whether people actually want to live, work, rent or operate businesses in the location.

Demand can vary significantly between neighbourhoods and property types. A location that is suitable for one purpose may not necessarily be suitable for another.

6. The Property Itself Still Matters

A good location does not automatically make every property a good purchase.

You should also consider the property's title, size, design, condition, layout, price, documentation, development status and other relevant factors.

7. Compare Similar Properties

Before deciding that a property is fairly priced, compare it with similar properties in the same or comparable locations.

Look at differences in size, property type, road access, infrastructure, documentation, development level and other important characteristics.

8. Consider Your Purpose

The right location depends partly on what you want to achieve.

A location suitable for a family home may not be the same location you would choose for a rental property, office, retail business or long-term land investment.

9. Think About the Present and the Future

Consider both the current condition of the area and credible information about planned development.

Do not rely solely on promises about future projects or expected appreciation. Where possible, verify important claims independently.

10. Location Does Not Guarantee Appreciation

A popular or developing location can be an important consideration, but no location guarantees that a property's value will increase.

Property values can be affected by economic conditions, supply and demand, infrastructure, government policies, market conditions and many other factors.

Location Evaluation Checklist

  • How accessible is the property?
  • What major roads and transport routes are nearby?
  • What important facilities are nearby?
  • What infrastructure currently exists?
  • What development is happening around the area?
  • What is the demand for this type of property?
  • How does the property compare with similar properties?
  • Does the location fit my purpose?
  • Which future-development claims can be independently verified?
  • Does the price make sense when compared with the property's features?

Final Thought

Location is an important part of property evaluation, but it is only one part of the bigger picture.

Look at the location together with the property, documentation, infrastructure, price, demand and your own objectives before making a decision.
